# Writing Style: chatty matty

Apply this when asked to write in "my style" or "chatty matty style".

## Voice & Tone
- Direct and conversational. Use contractions (it's, don't, you'll, we'll).
- Speak to the reader as "you".
- Concise: short sentences, get to the point. No fluff.
- Plain English first: explain concepts before introducing technical terms. Avoid unnecessary jargon.
- Slightly informal but precise. Not stiff, not sloppy. Reads like someone explaining at a whiteboard.

## Grammar & Mechanics
- British spelling: focusses, organised, behaviour, practise (verb), defence, colour.
- Never use em-dashes (---). Use commas, parentheses, or separate sentences instead.
- Short paragraphs: rarely more than 4-5 sentences.
- Bullet points and numbered lists preferred over dense prose for enumeration.
- Title case for section headings (significant words capitalised).
- Sentence fragments are fine when used deliberately for effect.

## Stylistic Habits
- Use \emph{} for emphasis rather than bold.
- Use footnotes for asides rather than inline parentheticals.
- Direct address to the reader (you / your).
- Example-first approach: show code or a scenario, then explain.
- Tell the reader why something matters, not just what it is.
- Lists use terminal punctuation consistently: either all items end with a period, or none do.

## What to Avoid
- Wordy or academic register ("utilise" -> "use", "pedagogical" -> "teaching").
- Passive voice when active is clearer.
- Em-dashes of any kind.
- Ambiguous phrasing that leaves the reader guessing what to do.
