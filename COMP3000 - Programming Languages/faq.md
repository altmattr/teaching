# COMP3000 FAQ

*Frequently asked questions about how the course works, based on what was said in the Week 1 2026 lecture.*

---

## Teams & Team-Based Learning

**Q: Are the assignments group work?**
No — every assessment in the course is individual. You submit your own work only. However, you'll be working on the assignments all semester in a team, and you're free to use as much of your teammates' work as you want in your submission.

**Q: So what's the point of the team?**
The team creates a common pool of work from which you draw your submission. Most teams do about 80% of their assignment work during the one-hour in-class sessions together. The rubric includes an item for "have you extended your team's work?" — so there's an incentive to go beyond what the team produced.

**Q: What is team-based learning (TBL)?**
Each week follows this cycle: do the pre-reading → sit the iRAT (individual multiple choice quiz) → sit the tRAT (same quiz, with your team) → FAT (classwide answer discussion) → Application Task (one hour of team work on something closely related to the assignment). It's designed to prime you to work with your teammates before hitting the main task.

**Q: What if someone in my team isn't contributing?**
The team writes a contract in week 1 laying out expectations. If someone disappears, the team can swap them out — it's not a big formal process. Teams handle it themselves, and classroom teachers are prepared to help if needed.

**Q: Are classes compulsory?**
Not formally, but your team needs you there. If you stop showing up, your team can replace you, and you'll have no team and no support for your assignment.

**Q: What if TBL just isn't for me?**
Nobody is forced into it, but everyone is encouraged to fight through the discomfort at first. If it's really not working, talk to the lecturer or Lucky and they'll sort something out.

**Q: How are teams formed?**
Geographically — sit next to someone you want to work with when you walk into class, and you'll likely end up on the same team. Friends groups tend to stay together because they sit together.

**Q: How will the group work relate to the assignment?**
The workshops are directly aligned with the assignment. Each week's application task is closely related to the assignment. So yes, you're building towards the assignment together as a team during those sessions. The team creates a common pool of work from which everyone draws their individual submission. You're free to use as much of your teammates' work as you want.

**Q: What if the team performs poorly?**
Two safety nets. First, you can fall back on your own work since it's an individual submission, so you're not entirely dependent on the team. Second, classroom teachers are watching for people stuck in non-functioning teams. If someone's the only one contributing, they'll likely be moved to another team. The team also writes a contract in week 1, and can swap out members who stop showing up.

## Log Books

**Q: What's a logbook?**
A personal record of what you did in each workshop class. Think of it as a good study and professional technique — the lecturer keeps one himself, writing down what he did each day and what's coming up.

**Q: Is it a team logbook?**
No — the logbook is personal, not a team logbook.

**Q: What format should it be in?**
Whatever works for you — electronic, paper, anything.

**Q: How do I get marks for it?**
Show your teacher your logbook at the end of each class. If you have entries that were done consistently through the semester and your teacher saw them, you get full marks. If you have some entries but they're inconsistent, you get partial marks. It's worth around 15–20% of the assignment assessment section.

**Q: What if I miss a week?**
Catch up — do a logbook entry for the work you missed when you get back.

**Q: Do the entries need to be detailed?**
Timeliness matters more than quality. We're only looking for a few notes.

**Q: Are they submitted with the assignment?**
Yes — they go in with your assignment submission, like a reflection you've already written.

**Q: Is there any other benefit?**
Yes — your teacher gives you feedback based on what's in your logbook, so you get timely feedback during the semester instead of waiting until marking.

**Q: How will the logbook contribute to the assignment?**
Two ways. First, there's a rubric item for logbook entries worth around 20% of the assignment assessment section — full marks for consistent entries your teacher saw, partial marks if they're patchy. Second, your teacher reads your logbook and gives you feedback throughout the semester, so you get 13 chances to get feedback on your assignment work rather than waiting until marking. The entries are submitted alongside your assignment submission.

**Q: Should the logbook be a record of events, or more about my personal feelings and opinions?**
Both — but in a practical, lightweight way. My own practice is to down what I did each day AND the decisions I made and why. For you, that means something like: "Worked on X. Tried Y approach because Z. Got stuck on W." Not a diary, not a deep personal reflection — just enough that you (and your teacher) can see you were thinking about what you were doing AND you can go back to make sense of what you did in the past and add that information to your submissions. Timeliness matters more than quality, so don't stress about making it polished. A few notes right after class is better than a long reflective essay a week later.

**Q: If I miss a class and write a logbook entry, is it fine if it doesn't talk about what happened in class?**
Yes — that should be fine. The emphasis is on timeliness over quality, and the point is to show you're engaging with the course, not to document what happened in class. An entry like "Missed class, caught up by doing X" still shows the study habit the logbook is designed to encourage. If you're worried, ask your teacher — they're the ones marking it.

## Assignments

**Q: How many assignments are there?**
Two submission points (Assignment 1 and Assignment 2), but they're both on the same major project.

**Q: What do I submit?**
The code, plus answers to questions about the code. It's not just the source code — there's a lot more in the submission than just the code.

**Q: Can I use AI?**
Yes — there are basically no restrictions. Just make sure you tell us what was AI-generated.

**Q: Will the workshops help with the assignment?**
Directly — the tasks in workshops are very closely related to the assignment. They'll probably be something that exactly appears in your assignment. The full assignment is released earlier than you'd expect.

**Q: When will we see the rubrics?**
Early — probably week 2. You won't be waiting until weeks 5 or 6.
