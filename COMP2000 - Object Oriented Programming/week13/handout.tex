\begin{abstract}
The final week is a conference-style showcase.  Each student who chooses to present will have 15 minutes plus 5 minutes of questions to present their simulation and the work they have done over the semester.  This is a celebration of what you have built and an opportunity to share your most interesting findings.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Topics}
\begin{description}
\item[Project presentation] Demonstrating a complete software project
\item[Design narrative] Telling the story of how your design evolved
\item[Emergent behaviour] Reflecting on what your simulation taught you
\end{description}

\section*{Preparation}
Attend the lecture in week 12.\par
Prepare a short presentation of your simulation.  Show it running, explain your class architecture, and highlight the most interesting emergent behaviour you observed.  You do not need slides --- running your code and walking through your design is sufficient.


\section*{EPIC activity}

\begin{note}
This is the second graded submission point.  Final log books, code, and reflections are due.
\end{note}

This is a \emph{conference} style class.  The activity and reporting time is devoted to individual presentations given by students.  Students register their interest (not all students need to present) and get 15 minutes plus 5 minutes question time to present their work.  All students are expected to be present, even if they choose not to present.  Class finishes when all interested students have presented.

\subsection*{Reporting}

All students will have been working on simulations over the semester so all can write reflections in their log books.  Your log book should include:
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0em
\item Notes from at least two presentations by other students.
\item One design idea or technique you saw that you wish you had tried.
\item A final reflection on your own project: what you learned, what you are proud of, and what you would do differently.
\end{itemize}

\begin{teacher}
Coordinate the presentation schedule in advance.  This is a celebratory final class---consider bringing snacks or arranging a prize for the most impressive simulation voted by the students.
\end{teacher}

\section*{Project Guidance}

This is your team's work \emph{between classes}, do it in your own time and have it committed and pushed to your shared repository before you return next week.  Your final submission is due this week, so use the time before the deadline to finalise it: fork the team repository, complete your worksheet, and make sure your log book entries and final reflection are in order.  Present your simulation to the class.  Demonstrate the most interesting emergent behaviour your ecosystem produces --- did a predator-prey cycle emerge naturally?  Did a particular species dominate?  Did the ecosystem stabilise or collapse?  Explain the key design decisions your team made along the way and what you learned from building it.
